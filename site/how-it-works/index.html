How a song becomes a kit.

kitCreator runs four real stages on your audio: it separates the mix into instruments, figures out every note that was played, groups similar-sounding hits together, and packages the result as a standard sampler instrument. Every stage below is backed by a named, open-source model — nothing here is a black box.

01

Separation

The full mix goes into a source-separation network that isolates drums, bass, vocals, and "other" into individual stems. For pitched instruments, a second pass strips residual vocal bleed first, since guitar/piano/synth content usually lives in Demucs's noisier "other" stem.

Demucs — Meta AI Research BS-RoFormer
02

Extraction

For drums: onset detection finds every individual hit in the isolated stem, sorts them by energy into velocity layers, and slices clean one-shots. For pitched instruments: a transcription model finds every note actually played, and each detected pitch becomes its own sampled zone.

Basic Pitch — Spotify CREPE (torchcrepe) librosa onset detection
03

Timbre matching

Every extracted hit is embedded into a shared timbre space, then clustered so the kit keeps only the most sonically distinct 2–4 round-robins per velocity layer — via farthest-point sampling — instead of dozens of near-identical takes of the same drum hit.

LAION-CLAP HDBSCAN
04

Export

Attack/decay/sustain/release and loop points are estimated per sample, then the whole kit is written out twice — once as an SFZ file, once as a DecentSampler preset — so it loads in essentially any free or paid sampler, no plugin required.

SFZ format DecentSampler Rubber Band DeepFilterNet
